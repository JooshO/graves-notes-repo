At the dawn of all things, there was a great Wheel. And at the center of that Wheel was a perfect divinity. And everything was, and will be. But after forever had come and gone, the divinity grew weary of this first wheel, simple and smooth. He drew together that which was, and she shattered the Wheel and themself all at once. From the broken divinity sprung the many Gods of our era, and from that shattered wheel, the earth and the sun and the stars. 

The Gods told stories, shaped heroes, raised and brought low societies. They are, in nature, fragments of that divinity - not quite perfect in their broken nature but together, in concourse, infallible. They made much of that creation. But Creation, though broken, is fundamentally formed of Wheel-stuff. And the Wheel may turn and turn and turn, but adrift in the cosmos it may not move and so no matter how scattered, the Gods and their shapes are returned to their original states. The tales are told again and again and again, with only small mortal variation. For, while the Gods are made of that broken divinity, of cold white flame, and their sculptures of earth and star are made of Wheel-stuff, we are twice removed from that nature. While the cosmic tale may be told and retold, we may find some freedom in the cracks.

And yet, even if we may be committed to the same part in the song of the stars told over and over again, we must find the strength to play that part to our best. Be it a happy tale or a tragedy, if we can find a way for it to turn out just a little better this time than that is enough.


### Pantheon
While the faiths of the wheel form an open pantheon that easily slots in other beliefs, most sects and groups form around a core set of poems, stories, and scripture. A pantheon, in that sense, is not an exhaustive list of gods but rather a set of main characters for those stories. This set of six is the primary group observed in the sect the Keeran belongs to.

**Kas** - Lady of the Well
Kas is usually depicted as a dark skinned woman carrying two buckets on a shoulder yoke. She is revered as the mother of the original followers of this faith, the name of that people group now lost to history (at least among the walking cities). She is frequently depicted as a kingmaker and a world shaper, someone slow to anger but who will destroy those who stray too far from her path.
Geological review of the home region of these peoples would reveal a primary river upon which many early settlements were built. That river dramatically changed route several times throughout early history, leaving some settlements dry and weak while making others prosper.

**Am Tyr** - Lord of the forge and the flame
Am Tyr is usually shown as a larger, muscular man, a bit on the stout side with calloused hands and heavy protective garb. He is a maker, and typically friendly with heroes, whom he aids with material gifts. The oldest stories about him describe him giving the gift of the hot flame to mankind that they might shape the world about him. Early contemporary corruptions and retellings (some of which source to the Myrarchi church itself) will occasionally put him in place of Ramidius as far as granting soul manipulation to the Myarchi people. This is likely a bastardization of the stories of Ramidius granting magic and Am Tyr teaching of the "fire within" (which later turned out to draw through similar arcane means).

**Wren** - Lady of the Ravens
Wren is typically depicted as a raven scaled up to the size of an eagle, although depending on the teller she can described as many passerine birds. She is, as many bird deities are, seen as an omen bringer and a storyteller. Occasionally she is also a direct messenger, but usually she brings divine portents rather than literal messages. Her song is said to be impossibly beautiful to those who are lucky enough to hear it.

**Am Reman** - Old Man Autumn
Am Reman is typically described as an old man cloaked in reds, oranges, and browns with a worn face and leathery skin. He leans upon a long staff twisted and gnarled, which when thrown breaks the sky as lightning and shakes the earth as thunder.
In some tales he acts as a harvest deity, but more frequently he warns of hardship and the coming winter. He brings the heavy fall winds that strip bark and leaves from trees. In most stories, he symbolizes a choice - hunker down or travel where it is warmer. Flee me or endure what comes after. Despite this, he is not feared - while his stories are fewer he is revered as a source of wisdom and a sign of the value of experience. 

**Am Peral** - Gatekeeper and Hearthkeeper. Lord of the Walled Garden
Am Peral is typically depicted as a tall and broad male figure.
He is often symbolized by brambles, nettles, and roses. In the stories, he usually serves the role of a guardian, but also a comforter, and symbolizes somewhere safe to return.

**Iyun** - Lady of the Cold White Flame (angelic)
Iyun is normally depicted as an incredibly tall and thin figure, often marble white and always cloaked in a white fire. She acts as a sort of reclusive matriarch in most stories, only appearing when the threat is truly great. As a result, very little texts and stories survive to the modern day since the apocalypse has already come about for those in and around Myrarchi culture. She is typically a kind of destroying angel whose gifts serve to cut out the undead and annihilate the tyrant.
